What it is

Our technique, not a ready-made routine

Sculptural massage, also called contouring massage, belongs to the same family as myoplastic massage: deeper, targeted work on the facial muscles and fascia, closer to physiotherapy holds than to a beauty treatment. Our version is a signature one - a technique developed by our own team.

The treatment has two goals: shaping the face contour and smoothing out expression tension, meaning the way your face habitually tightens - a furrowed forehead, drawn brows, a clenched jaw. We start by removing make-up and cleansing the skin, and adapt the treatment to what you come in with.

FAQ

Sculptural facial massage - common questions

How is sculptural massage different from Kobido?

Kobido is a Japanese technique with characteristic, very fast and rhythmic movements of both hands. Sculptural massage works more slowly and more precisely, on the muscles and fascia. Our signature version belongs to the second group and focuses on the face contour and expression tension.

What does "signature" mean?

That it is a technique developed by our team. Its goal is always the same - the contour and expression tension - and we adapt the details to each face.

Will I see a result after one session?

After one visit the face usually looks rested and the expression is relaxed. A more lasting change in the contour comes with a series and with what happens between visits - sleep, hydration and whether you clench your teeth.

Can it replace aesthetic medicine?

No. Massage works on tension, circulation and puffiness. It does not restore lost volume or remove structural wrinkles, the ones that stay visible even when the face is completely relaxed.

How often should I have it?

When working on the contour, a series of 6–10 treatments every 7–14 days is usually suggested, followed by maintenance visits every 3–4 weeks.

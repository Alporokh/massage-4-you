What it is

A massage planned around your training, not the calendar

We start a sports massage by asking what you do and when. Working with someone who has a race in three days is different from working with someone who has just finished the season. We ask about your sport, training volume, old injuries and which area speaks up first when the load goes up.

Then we work where your sport takes its toll: for runners usually the calves, thighs and hips, at the gym the back and shoulder girdle, for cyclists the lower back and neck. We warm up, release the deeper layers, add stretching if needed - and keep the pressure at the edge of comfort, not beyond it.

Contraindications

When to postpone your visit

Fever or infection
Fresh injury and swelling
Torn muscle or ligament
Thrombosis or vein disease
Advanced cancer
First trimester of pregnancy (ask us first)

A fresh injury is not a job for massage. If something snapped during training, swelled up or hurts with every movement - see a doctor or physiotherapist first. Come back to us with a diagnosis; then working around the injured area makes sense and is safe.

FAQ

Sports massage - frequently asked questions

Is a sports massage only for athletes?

No. The name describes the way of working, not who is on the table. Runners and gym-goers come for it, but so do people who walk a lot, lift or do physical work. What matters is that the muscles are loaded regularly and need support with recovery.

How long before a race can I come in?

We usually do a pre-race massage 2–3 days before the event and keep it lighter, so the muscles have time to settle. We avoid hard, deep work right before a start - it can leave you sore the next day. After a race, it is best to come 24–48 hours later.

Does a sports massage hurt?

It can be intense, but it should not be painful. We work at the edge of comfort, which we agree with you as we go - a massage that makes you grit your teeth tightens the muscles instead of releasing them. We adjust the pressure during the session; just say a word.

How is it different from a deep tissue massage?

Deep tissue is a way of working - slow, focused pressure on the deeper layers. A sports massage is a goal: preparing or recovering a body that trains. It often uses deep techniques, but adds faster warming up, stretching and work on the muscle groups your sport loads.

How often should I come?

With regular training, every 2–3 weeks works well. During heavier training blocks or race preparation some people come every week. If you train recreationally, once a month is usually enough to stop tension building up.
